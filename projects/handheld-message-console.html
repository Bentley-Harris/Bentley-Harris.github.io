Handheld Message Console

2026 · Electronics & Embedded
  • Arduino
  • OLED
  • 3D printing
  • Soldering

A handheld device I designed and built as a gift, roughly the shape and feel of a Game Boy. It holds 365 messages, one for each day of the year, which you scroll through with physical buttons. Any message can be marked as a favourite and revisited later from its own list.

[One sentence on who it was for and the occasion.]

Key details

  • Designed the enclosure in CAD and 3D printed it, sized around the display, board and battery.
  • OLED display, physical buttons for navigation, and a buzzer giving audio feedback on presses and menu changes.
  • Wrote a full menu system: scroll through 365 unique messages, mark any of them as a favourite, and open a separate favourites list to read them again.
  • Added a settings menu for sound and screen brightness.
  • Battery monitoring with a live voltage readout, so the device shows how much charge is left rather than simply dying.
  • Built the circuit on a fully soldered board and finished it to a standard I was happy to hand over as a gift.

What I learned

Most of the work was in the firmware, not the hardware. Getting a menu to feel right with only a few buttons took far longer than wiring the parts together: every mode needs an obvious way back out, and the device has to survive someone pressing buttons in an order I never anticipated without ending up stuck in a state it cannot leave.

The other lesson was physical. Designing an enclosure around components is very different from designing one in isolation, and the tolerances that matter are the ones you only find after the first print.
