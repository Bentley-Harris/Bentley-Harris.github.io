Fish Tank Automation System

2026 · Electronics & Embedded
  • Arduino
  • MOSFET switching
  • RTC
  • 3D printing

An automation system for my fish tank, currently in its final stages. It runs the tank light on a schedule and feeds the fish automatically, both driven by an Arduino with a menu on a small OLED display. I built it because the tank needed a routine that did not depend on me remembering.

Key details

  • Reused the tank's existing light instead of replacing it: the Arduino switches it through MOSFETs on an automated on/off schedule.
  • Designed and 3D printed an automatic fish feeder, which has now run reliably for more than thirty days.
  • OLED display and a real-time clock module, with a scrollable menu and several operating modes.
  • Built and tested an earlier variant with RF remote control. It worked and I have footage of it, but it was cut from the final design.
  • [Why you dropped the RF remote. Explaining a decision to remove something working says more than the feature would have.]
  • Still on a breadboard. The next steps are moving it to a soldered perfboard build and designing a 3D printed enclosure for the electronics.

What I learned

Reliability matters far more than features on anything that runs unattended. The feeder has kept going for over a month, and that is mostly because the mechanism is simple — earlier versions had more moving parts and found more ways to jam.

Removing the RF remote was the right call even though it worked. Building something and then cutting it is not wasted time; I would rather have tested the idea than wondered about it.
