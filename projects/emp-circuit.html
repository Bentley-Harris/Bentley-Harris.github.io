EMP Circuit

2026 · Electronics & Embedded
  • High voltage
  • Energy storage
  • Experiment

A high-voltage experiment in energy storage and fast switching. A boost converter charges a bank of capacitors, and that stored energy is then released through a coil of magnet wire to produce a brief electromagnetic pulse. I built two versions and filmed both.

The write-up below is deliberately descriptive. It covers what the circuit did and what I took from building it, not how to reproduce it.

Key details

  • Two versions built and tested.
  • Energy stored in a high-voltage capacitor bank charged by a boost converter, then discharged through a wound coil.
  • The real engineering problem is the switching rather than the storage: the pulse depends on getting the energy out quickly, not simply on how much is held.
  • Effects were clearly visible — arcing at discharge, and disruption of a calculator placed near the coil.
  • Documented on video across both versions.
  • No component values, wiring detail or build steps are published here, and I do not intend to publish them. It is a high-voltage circuit, and the interesting part for anyone reading this is the reasoning, not the recipe.

Video

What I learned

The lesson was about how fast stored energy can move, not about the pulse itself. Switching speed dominated everything: the same stored energy released slowly does almost nothing, and released quickly it is obvious across the room.

It also set how I handle high voltage generally. Working on this taught me to rely on a fixed process — discharge, verify, then touch — rather than on remembering what state the circuit was in.
