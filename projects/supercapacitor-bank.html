Supercapacitor Bank

2025 · Electronics & Embedded
  • Arduino
  • Voltage monitoring
  • OLED
  • Machining

This was the first electronics project I took all the way to finished rather than leaving on a breadboard. It is a bank of supercapacitors with a microcontroller watching every cell individually, an OLED reporting what each one is doing, and terminals I made by hand. I built it to understand how stored energy behaves when it can be released quickly.

Key details

  • Microcontroller-controlled charging, with the voltage of each individual capacitor monitored rather than only the pack as a whole.
  • OLED display showing per-cell voltages alongside overcharge warnings and status indicator LEDs.
  • Controlled discharge into a load, producing large arcs and enough energy to vaporise thin metal.
  • Custom clamp terminals machined by hand from scrap aluminium, because nothing I had on the shelf would carry that current at the connection.
  • Treated as a high-energy build throughout. The monitoring and the warnings exist because a supercapacitor bank does not behave like a battery and gives very little warning when something is wrong.

What I learned

The thing that made this project worth building was monitoring each cell instead of the pack. Cells do not charge evenly, and a single pack-level reading hides that completely — one cell can be sitting well above the others while the total looks perfectly normal.

It also changed how I work. Almost everything I have built since has some way of telling me what it is doing internally, because working blind on something that stores real energy is not a trade I want to make.
