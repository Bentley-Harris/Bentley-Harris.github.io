Supercapacitor Bank

2025 · Electronics & Embedded
  • Arduino
  • Voltage monitoring
  • OLED
  • Machining

This was the first electronics project I took all the way to finished rather than leaving on a breadboard. It is a bank of supercapacitors with an Arduino Nano monitoring every cell individually, a 0.96-inch OLED reporting what each one is doing, and aluminium terminals I made by hand from scrap metal. I built it to understand how stored energy behaves when it can be released quickly (and because I found it interesting to experiment with rapid discharge and its effects on materials).

Key details

  • Microcontroller-controlled charging, with the voltage of each individual capacitor monitored rather than only the pack as a whole.
  • OLED display showing per-cell voltages, overcharge warnings, and the rate of charge (dV/dt) for each capacitor and for the bank as a whole. Status LEDs show charging, near full, full, safe and primed to discharge.
  • Controlled discharge into a load, producing bright flashes and enough energy to vaporise thin metal wire.
  • Custom clamp terminals machined by hand from scrap aluminium.
  • Treated as a high-energy build throughout. The monitoring and the warnings exist because I wanted to learn how to build a system that was not only fun to experiment with, but also informative and safe, so I always knew what was happening internally.

What I learned

The thing that made this project worth building was monitoring each cell instead of the pack, and developing an informative UI. The capacitors in the bank did not charge evenly, and a single pack-level reading hides that completely. One cell can be sitting well above the others while the total looks perfectly normal, and the last thing I want is to overcharge a high-energy capacitor bank in my bedroom.

It also changed how I work. Almost everything I have built since has some way of telling me what it is doing internally, because working blind on something that stores real energy is honestly a bit scary. It is like driving with your eyes closed.
