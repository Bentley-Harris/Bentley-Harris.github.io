LED Heart

2025–2026 · Electronics & Embedded
  • Arduino
  • 555 timer
  • PWM
  • Soldering

An LED heart I built as a gift, first driven by an Arduino. I am now working on version 2, which drops the microcontroller completely and runs on two 555 timers instead.

Version 1: Arduino

  • An Arduino Nano driving a heart of red 5 mm LEDs, soldered onto perfboard and mounted over a clear acrylic backing plate.
  • Built-in patterns, including a light chase, flashing, blinking and solid.
  • Every LED ran to ground through one shared resistor, which turned out to be a mistake (more on that below).

Version 2: two 555 timers (in progress)

For version 2 I am ditching the microcontroller and using only analogue components.

  • Two 555 timers, each with its own potentiometer. The first generates a PWM signal that switches the LEDs through a MOSFET. Turning its pot changes the on/off ratio, so the user controls the brightness.
  • The second 555 runs the flashing mode, and its pot sets the flash speed. On the breadboard it went from about one flash every three seconds up to a fast flash at the top of the range.
  • A switch changes between solid and flashing modes. The brightness control still works while it is flashing, so the two circuits work together.
  • 26 red 3 mm LEDs, a lot more than version 1.
  • Every LED has its own resistor to ground, so they all light evenly. That adds up to more than 100 solder joints on a tightly packed board, which is a big part of why it is not finished yet.
  • Prototyped on breadboards first. Everything worked, although the dimming was a bit finicky and had a cut-off near the bottom of its range.

What I learned

Version 1 taught me not to assume. I ran every LED to ground through one shared resistor and assumed it would be fine as long as the resistor could handle the wattage. In practice, small differences between the LEDs meant some took more of the current than others, so some were much brighter than the rest and the heart lit unevenly. In version 2, every LED gets its own resistor.
